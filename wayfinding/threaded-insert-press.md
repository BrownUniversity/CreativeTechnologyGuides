# Threaded-insert press

### Locations:&#x20;

* Physical Media Lab, Granoff Center, Room N310

The threaded-insert press is designed to help with the installation of threaded inserts into 3D printed parts. Threaded inserts can make 3D printed parts stronger and can last longer. For small screws they can also be easier to use than trying to design fine threads in 3D printed parts.

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/ThreadedInsertPress_WithInset (1).png" alt="The threaded insert press and soldering iron. Inset image of finished print with heated insert."><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

The press uses a soldering iron fitted with a special tip designed to fit the inserts.

### Designing for threaded inserts

Before using the press you must design a hole for the threaded insert in your 3D print. Each insert is different but the diameter of the hole should be just big enough for the edge of the insert to fit. The texture part of the insert should not fit into the hole as the goal is to melt the plastic around it to get a tight fit.

### Getting Started:

#### 1. Turn on the iron and set the temperature

Turn the soldering iron on to approximately 410° F This is the approximate melting temperature of PLA. Use the arrow buttons to change the temperature up and down. Remember the metal tip of the iron will be very hot.

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/ThreadedInsertPress_Temp (1).png" alt="Soldering iron set to 410 degrees F on the left. Picture of metal tip of iron on right use caution as the entire metal tip is hot."><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### 2. Place the 3D-print and heated insert

Place the 3D-printed part below the iron and set the insert into the 3D printed part. There is usually a slightly tapered end to the insert to help you place it in the part.

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/ThreadedInsertPress_Steps.png" alt="Four images of part. First without insert. Second insert placed. Third iron pressing insert. Fourth completed part with insert."><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### 3. Press the insert into the part

Press gently but firmly until the insert sits flush with the surface of the 3D printed part. Pull up the iron and your done. If the thread isn't quite flush you can press down again.

#### 4. Turn off the soldering iron when you are done!
